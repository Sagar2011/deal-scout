Risks and open questions

  • Regulatory risk: small-dollar consumer lending to underserved US borrowers is heavily regulated at federal and state levels (e.g., state usury caps, CFPB oversight); cross-border team (Canada) adds compliance complexity.
  • Credit risk: serving underserved borrowers implies elevated default rates; underwriting model performance is unverified in public evidence.
  • Capital risk: lending requires balance-sheet funding; no evidence of warehouse facility, bank partnership, or securitization capacity.
  • Concentration risk: founding team of three with limited prior US consumer-lending operating experience at scale.
  • Competition risk: incumbent fintech lenders (e.g., OppFi, Possible Finance, LendUp successors) and bank-affiliated digital lenders.
  • Execution risk: very early stage (3 employees, founded 2024) — no evidence of unit economics, default rates, or customer cohorts.
  • Geographic/structural risk: Canadian-incorporated entity lending to US borrowers raises licensing and enforceability questions.
